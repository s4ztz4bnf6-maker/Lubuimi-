# Research note: the missing cylinder-mass identity

Date: 2026-10-23

## Exact target

The finite coordinate model already has:
- a finite product space of coordinate values;
- an explicitly factorized probability distribution;
- an exact coordinatewise cylinder-sum identity;
- an exact criterion for two coordinate cylinders to intersect;
- a finite union/survival lemma.

The missing bridge is not another generic inequality. It is the theorem identifying the mass of the whole cylinder with the product of its fixed-coordinate weights.

For a coordinate-weight system w and cylinder H, the target is

  μ_w(H) = product over i in H.fixed of w_i(H.value_i),

where μ_w is the genuine product distribution generated by w. Free coordinates contribute one because their coordinate weights sum to one.

This identity is stronger and more useful than an intersection-zero lemma: it supplies exact masses for compatible cylinders, after which the second-moment or distortion estimates can use the actual overlap pattern.

## Proof architecture

Write the indicator of H as the product of coordinate indicators:
  1_H(x) = product over i of 1_constraint_i(x_i),
where a fixed coordinate contributes the indicator of its prescribed value and a free coordinate contributes 1.

Then the weighted indicator factors:
  μ_w(x) 1_H(x) = product over i of [w_i(x_i) 1_constraint_i(x_i)].

Sum over the finite product space and use the finite product-sum identity:
  sum over x of product over i of g_i(x_i) = product over i of sum over y_i of g_i(y_i).

For a fixed coordinate, its factor sum collapses to the prescribed weight. For a free coordinate, it is the normalization sum 1. This yields the cylinder-mass formula.

The critical Lean detail is to make the indicator factorization explicit rather than ask simplification to infer independence from a sample-space shape. The existing cylinderCoordinateSum_eq and cylinderCoordinateSums_product_eq establish the coordinatewise half; the full theorem must still connect the filtered event sum to the product of local sums.

## A non-product alternative

If the source's stage distribution is not factorized, the cylinder formula is unavailable. In that case one must prove a separate conditional/relative distortion bound for the actual joint measure. A normalized distribution on a finite product does not imply coordinate independence. This is a hard boundary, not a Lean technicality.

## Error budget integration

Once the cylinder-mass formula is established, define the bad event from actual geometric conflicts, derive its mass from the compatible/incompatible intersection structure, and only then instantiate the stage error eta_s and factor C_s. The affine TransferBudget composition is useful only after these constants are grounded in the source model.

The final survival conclusion requires a strict source-derived bound below one (or below the actual survival margin of the relevant theorem). No such BBMST-specific budget is claimed in this note.
